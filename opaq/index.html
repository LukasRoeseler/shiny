Welcome to the Open Anchoring Quest

Research on anchoring effects is currently challenged by the lack of reliability of anchoring scores. With OpAQ we tackle this problem by meta-analytically investigating different measures of anchoring susceptibility and the factors influencing their reliability — using an open, free and ever-growing item-level dataset of thousands of trials from anchoring experiments.

What is OpAQ?

An ever-growing, free, open, multi-study, item-level dataset that lets researchers solve current challenges in anchoring-effects research. Susceptibility to anchoring on one item is almost completely uncorrelated with susceptibility on any other item — OpAQ helps investigate why.

Status

This project was run until early 2023 and is not actively maintained anymore. All materials are available under a CC-BY 4.0 license.

What can you do?

Explore the dataset and the dynamic meta-analysis on anchoring effect sizes and reliabilities. Test additional hypotheses such as file-drawer effects, anchor extremeness, or age effects. Download the full dataset from the Dataset tab or from OSF.

Reliabilities of Susceptibility to Anchoring Scores

Despite over a decade of personality-moderator research, most anchoring scores are extremely unreliable (e.g., Cronbach's Alphas below .5 in many cases). Here you can see the reliability (average inter-item correlations) of different susceptibility scores from different studies.

Distance Between Anchor and True Value

The distance between the anchor and the true value affects anchoring-effect strength. Relative distance of anchor is (anchor − true value) / true value. Relative adjustment is (estimate − true value) / (anchor − true value). Studies without a true value have no points.
